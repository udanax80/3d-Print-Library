    Beginner Buying

    What to buy¶

    Buy the smallest machine that fits the parts you will print this month, in the material you will actually use. PLA on a simple printer teaches you more than a flagship that sits in the box.

    The dated spec table is Printer comparison. This page is how to read it. Numbers below that name a price are the from-prices or sale prices we copied on 2026-11-07. They will change. Confirm them on the maker's site.

    Printer types¶

    Open-frame, bed moves. The Bambu US store calls the A1 series open-frame. The bed or the gantry slides toward you. They are the usual first printer: easy to see, easy to fix, and a poor home for ABS. Tall thin parts can wobble because the bed is moving.

    CoreXY. The bed usually only moves up and down. Two belts share the X and Y motion. Creality's K1C page, Elegoo's Centauri Carbon page, Anycubic's Kobra S1 page, Flashforge's AD5X page, and Prusa's CORE One+ page all say CoreXY. Many of these are enclosed. The motion type is not a quality score.

    Enclosed. Walls and a door. They hold heat, which ABS, ASA, nylon, and polycarbonate want, and they keep hands off a fast head. PLA often wants the door open so the box does not get hot enough to jam. "Enclosed" on a spec sheet is not the same thing as a heated chamber.

    Heated chamber. The box has its own heater and a published maximum, often 55–65 °C on the machines that state one. That is what you want for large nylon or PC parts. Leftover heat from the bed is weaker and usually unstated. If our table shows a dash, the page we read did not give a number.

    Multi-color: filament changer. One nozzle, several spools. Bambu's store describes AMS and AMS Lite combos. Creality Hi uses CFS (4 spools, up to 16). Flashforge AD5X has a 4-color IFS built in. Every color change purges plastic into a wipe tower or a chute. It is slower and messier than the photos suggest, and soft TPU is often not allowed.

    Tool changer. The printer parks extra toolheads and picks one up. Snapmaker U1 and Flashforge Creator 5 Pro ship with four. Bambu's H3C spec page says SwapTek 9-tool switching. Less purge, more mechanics, more money.

    IDEX. Two independent heads. Snapmaker J1s can print two materials or copy and mirror a part. The bed gets narrower in copy mode. The spec page lists the smaller volumes.

    Resin, briefly. A vat of liquid, a screen, and UV light. Beautiful miniatures. Not a first printer: nitrile gloves, isopropyl wash, a cure station, and a ventilated corner. Buy one later if you already like the process. None of the rows in the comparison table are resin machines.

    Budget, from prices we actually saw¶

    These are buckets for the 2026-11-07 prices in the table, printer-only or the from-price, not a combo unless the note says so.

    What we saw Examples on that day Reasonable hope
    Around $200–$300 A1 mini from $199, A1 from $259 PLA and PETG, one color, learn the first layer
    Around $350–$600 Kobra S1 sale $349, AD5X sale $349, Centauri Carbon $438, P2S from $449, X2D from $589 Enclosure, or four colors, or a second nozzle. Read which one you are paying for
    Around $1,000–$1,300 K2 Plus lower listed price $999, MK4S from $999, H2S from $1,049, CORE One+ from $1,299 Bigger volume, a heated chamber, or a Prusa you can repair for years
    About $1,400 and up H2D from $1,399, H2C from $1,899, H3C from $2,499 Dual nozzle or a tool changer on a large heated machine

    A combo (printer plus the color unit) can add a few hundred dollars. The table's price note says when the figure is printer-only.

    What to look for¶

    • Build volume in millimeters, larger than your part. A 180 mm cube (A1 mini) is a lot of useful objects. A 350 mm cube (K2 Plus) is a different piece of furniture.
    • Nozzle temperature. 300 °C covers PLA, PETG, ABS, and many nylons. 350 °C shows up on the H2 series and the K2 Plus. Only chase it if you have a filament that asks for it.
    • Enclosure and a chamber number, if you want ABS or nylon. "Enclosed" without a chamber temperature still blocks drafts. It may not hold 60 °C.
    • Who changes the filament. Built-in (AD5X), optional and extra (AMS, CFS, MMU3), or several toolheads. Count the slots on the product page. Our table leaves a dash when the page did not say.
    • A hardened nozzle if the marketing mentions carbon fiber. Brass wears out on those filaments.
    • A parts story. Prusa sells kits and spare parts and documents the MK4S and CORE One+ in public. Closed machines are fine until a $40 fan is out of stock. Check that before you depend on it.

    Common mistakes¶

    • Buying a multi-color flagship as a first printer, then fighting purges before you have printed a clean cube.
    • Trusting "500 mm/s" or "1000 mm/s" as the speed of a nice-looking part. Several makers also publish a lower "typical" or "print" speed. The table's speed note says which number we copied.
    • Assuming every enclosed printer can print ABS. Read the filament list. The A1 spec pages say ABS is not recommended.
    • Ignoring the room. ABS and nylon need ventilation. See Safety. A closed bedroom is a bad print farm.
    • Skipping the plate wash and then returning a printer that was fine.
    • Buying last year's model from a marketplace because a blog still calls it current. The X1 Carbon was not on Bambu's US collection page on 2026-11-07. The table leaves its price empty on purpose.
