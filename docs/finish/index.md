# Finish: post-processing

## Support removal
- Use flush cutters, needle-nose pliers, and a deburring tool. Wear **eye protection**, since PLA shards fly.
- Remove PA supports **within ~2 hours** of printing, before they absorb moisture ([Bambu Wiki: Support](https://wiki.bambulab.com/en/software/bambu-studio/support)).
- Soluble interfaces: PVA/BVOH in warm water, HIPS in d-limonene.

## Sanding & filling
Prusa's step-by-step approach ([Prusa blog: Postprocessing step by step](https://blog.prusa3d.com/postprocessing-of-3d-prints-step-by-step_29270/)):

1. Rough sand (~P100–P200) to knock down layer lines and seams.
2. Fill gaps with filler or filler primer. Repeat filler and sanding until smooth.
3. Finish with ~P400+, **wet sanding** for PLA so heat and friction don't melt and clog the paper.
4. Prime → paint → clear coat.

## Chemical smoothing
- **ABS and ASA** smooth with **acetone vapor** for a glossy, molded look, but sharp details soften and thin parts can deform. ([Prusa blog: Chemical smoothing](https://blog.prusa3d.com/improve-your-3d-prints-with-chemical-smoothing_36268/))
- PLA and PETG **don't** smooth with acetone.
- Solvents are flammable and their vapors are hazardous. Work outdoors or under extraction, away from ignition sources. See [Safety](../safety/index.md).

## Other finishes
- **Heat-set inserts:** see [Design → Threads & inserts](../design/index.md#threads-inserts-fasteners).
- **Annealing** (PLA, PA): can raise heat resistance and strength but causes shrinkage and warping. Test on a sample and scale the model to compensate.
- **Epoxy coating:** fills layer lines and adds gloss and strength on decorative parts.
- **Heat gun:** quickly removes stringing hairs and restores color in stress-whitened PLA. Pass briefly, don't linger.
- **Gluing:** CA (super glue) for PLA/PETG/ABS. Acetone weld for ABS/ASA. Use a plastic-rated epoxy for mixed materials.
