# Materials 101

Start with **PLA**. It is stiff, it likes a cool breeze, and a failed print is cheap. Move to **PETG** when you want something tougher. Try **TPU** when you want it bendy. Leave **ABS** and **ASA** until you have an enclosure and a plan for the smell.

The ranges below are the same starting points as the [material matrix](index.md), checked against Prusa's material articles and Bambu's filament guide. **Type the number from your spool first.** A silk PLA or a "high flow" PETG can sit outside the band.

Dry times are for a filament dryer or a warm oven you trust not to melt the spool. When in doubt, use the dryer's preset for that plastic.

## How to choose

| You need | Buy |
|---|---|
| A first spool, a model, a clip that lives indoors | PLA |
| A bracket that gets bumped, or a bit more heat than PLA | PETG |
| A grip, a foot, a hinge | TPU, and check that your filament changer allows that softness |
| A part in the sun | ASA, enclosed, ventilated |
| A part near heat, once the easy plastics are boring | The "Later" tab: nylon or PC, dry, enclosed |

PLA and PETG barely stick to each other. That is annoying in a part and useful when one of them is only support. See [multi-material planning](../prepare/multi-material.md).

More temperatures, drying, and failure notes: [material matrix](index.md) and [drying and storage](drying-storage.md).
