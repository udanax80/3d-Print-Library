# Start here

You do not need a workshop. A desk, a spool of PLA, and one afternoon is enough for a first print.

Work through these six steps in order. Each one has a checklist. When a step points at another page, that page goes deeper. You can skip the deep page until you need it.

## 1. What 3D printing is

A filament printer (FDM, also called FFF) melts a plastic string and draws the object in thin layers. The picture on your screen is a hollow shell with a lattice inside, not a solid block, unless you ask for that.

Resin printers shine light through a screen into liquid resin. They make finer detail and they are a worse first machine: gloves, washing, and curing, plus fumes. This site is about filament printers. Resin gets one mention in the [buying guide](../buy/index.md) so you know it exists.

The words you will see immediately: [nozzle](../glossary.md#nozzle), [filament](../glossary.md#filament), [slicer](../glossary.md#slicer), [build plate](../glossary.md#build-plate). The [glossary](../glossary.md) has the rest.

- [ ] I can say, in one sentence, that the printer stacks melted plastic.
- [ ] I know this site's filament pages are the path, and resin is a different hobby.

## 2. Choose a first printer

Buy for the things you will actually print in the next month, not for a spec you might want in a year.

For almost everyone that means PLA on an open-frame machine, or a small enclosed machine if you already know you want ABS. Multicolor is fun and it is also a second thing to learn. You can add it later on many printers.

Read [what to buy](../buy/index.md), then the [printer table](../buy/printers.md). Prices there are dated. A dash means the maker's page did not say.

- [ ] I wrote down the largest part I want, in millimeters, and the materials (start with PLA).
- [ ] I picked a printer whose build volume is bigger than that part.
- [ ] I checked today's price on the maker's site. The table can be a week old.

## 3. Unbox, level, and get a first layer

Most new printers arrive mostly built. Budget an hour, not a weekend. Follow the maker's card, not a random video of a different model.

The only skill that matters today is the [first layer](../glossary.md#first-layer). Lines should look like a flat ribbon, stuck to each other, not a round thread and not a transparent smear.

- [ ] The printer is on a sturdy table, with space behind it for the bed or the door.
- [ ] I ran the on-screen setup: update, load filament, auto-level if it has it.
- [ ] I washed the build plate with dish soap and water before the first print. New plates ship with a film of oil.
- [ ] I watched a one-layer square or the printer's built-in first-layer test and adjusted [Z-offset](../glossary.md#z-offset) until the lines joined.

If the plastic will not stick, use the [bed adhesion](../fix/index.md#bed-adhesion) checks before you change anything else.

## 4. Print the sample that came with the printer

The printer's own sample is sliced for that machine. Use it. A model from the internet adds slicer choices you have not practiced yet.

Use the PLA that came in the box, or a fresh dry spool. Load it the way the screen says. Start the print and stay for the first ten minutes.

- [ ] Filament is seated, and the end is cut straight, not crushed.
- [ ] The nozzle and bed reached the temperatures on the screen before it started moving.
- [ ] I stayed through the first layer. It stuck.
- [ ] I let the part cool a little, then flexed the plate to pop it off. I did not pry with a metal knife into a PEI sheet.

A failed sample is still useful. Match what you see to the [troubleshooting index](../fix/index.md) and change one thing.

## 5. The slicer in ten minutes

A [slicer](../glossary.md#slicer) turns a model file into instructions. You only need ten settings to leave the sample behind. They are listed, in order, on [Slicer basics](../prepare/slicer-basics.md).

Download the slicer your printer's maker recommends (Bambu Studio, PrusaSlicer, Creality Print, OrcaSlicer, or the one named in the manual). Pick the printer profile that matches your model, not a similar one.

- [ ] The slicer has my exact printer and a 0.4 mm nozzle, unless I bought a different size.
- [ ] I opened one small STL, used the default PLA profile, and looked at the preview.
- [ ] I can point at the walls, the infill, and the first layer in that preview.

## 6. What to print next

Print things you will use, small enough to finish in an hour.

Good second prints: a drawer divider, a cable clip, a scraper handle, a simple vase with the "vase mode" or spiral setting if you want to see the nozzle work. Skip articulated dragons and multi-day statues until a one-hour part looks clean.

Where to get files, and what the license lets you do, is on [model sources](../choose/model-sources.md).

- [ ] I downloaded one model with a stated license and saved the link and the designer.
- [ ] I sliced it at 0.20 mm layers, with a brim if it has small feet.
- [ ] I watched the first layer again.
- [ ] I wrote down what I would change next time (temperature, speed, or orientation) instead of changing five settings at once.

When that part is in your hand, the rest of this site is a reference, not a course. [Materials 101](../materials/beginner.md) is the next page the first time you buy a different spool. [Maintenance](../maintain/index.md) is the page the first time the printer has a week of dust on it.
