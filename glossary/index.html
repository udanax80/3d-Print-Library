    Glossary¶

    Short definitions for this site. If a slicer uses a different word, it is usually the same idea.

    The same list is data/glossary.json, in alphabetical order of the term you see here.

    3MF¶

    A model or project file that can include plates, colors, and slicer settings. A Bambu 3MF may not print correctly on another brand until you re-slice it.

    ABS¶

    A strong filament that warps unless the air around it stays warm. It gives off styrene. Print it enclosed and ventilated.

    AMS / CFS / MMU¶

    Filament changers. One nozzle, several spools, and a purge each time the color changes. Bambu AMS, Creality CFS, and Prusa MMU3 are examples. Slot counts differ by model.

    ASA¶

    Like ABS, but it holds up better in sunlight. Same need for an enclosure and the same fume caution.

    Bed leveling¶

    Making the nozzle the same height above the plate everywhere it prints. Mesh leveling measures a grid of points and bends the path to follow a slightly warped bed.

    Bedslinger¶

    A printer whose bed slides in Y while the nozzle moves in X and Z. The moving bed can wobble tall prints. The Bambu A1 series is this general layout; their spec pages say open-frame rather than the word bedslinger.

    Bowden¶

    The extruder motor is away from the nozzle and feeds filament through a PTFE tube. Lighter head, fussier with soft filament.

    Bridge¶

    A horizontal span between two already-printed points. The slicer usually slows down and adds cooling so the strand can hang.

    Brim¶

    Extra loops of plastic stuck to the first layer, around the part, to keep corners down. You peel it off afterward.

    Build plate¶

    The flat surface the part sticks to. Often a spring-steel sheet with a PEI coating, clipped or magneted to the heated bed.

    Cartesian¶

    Motion along three straight axes. Prusa labels the MK4S Cartesian. People also use the word for bedslingers.

    Clog¶

    A blockage in the nozzle or hotend. The extruder clicks, and the print goes hollow. Heat creep, debris, or a nozzle too close to the plate are common causes.

    Cold pull¶

    A way to yank debris out of a nozzle: heat, feed a bit of filament, cool partway, and pull. The exact temperatures depend on the hotend. Use your printer's guide.

    CoreXY¶

    A motion style where the bed usually moves only in Z and two belts move the head in X and Y. Common on faster enclosed machines.

    Desiccant¶

    The drying beads in a spool box or AMS. They fill up. Recharge or replace them when the humidity card says so.

    Direct drive¶

    The extruder motor is on the toolhead, close to the nozzle. Better for flexible filament. A bit more moving weight.

    Elephant's foot¶

    The bottom layers bulge outward. The nozzle was too close, or the bed was hot enough to keep those layers soft.

    Enclosure¶

    Walls and a lid around the print. They hold heat and block drafts, which ABS and nylon need, and they keep fingers off a moving head.

    Extruder¶

    The gears that push filament into the hotend. Direct drive sits on the moving head. A Bowden extruder sits back on the frame and pushes through a tube.

    FDM / FFF¶

    Fused deposition modeling, also called fused filament fabrication. A printer melts plastic filament and draws the part one layer at a time.

    Filament¶

    The plastic strand the printer melts. Almost all desktop FDM machines use 1.75 mm filament.

    First layer¶

    The first pass of plastic on the plate. If this layer is even and stuck, most other problems get easier.

    Flow rate¶

    How much plastic the slicer asks for, as a percentage of the calculated amount. Also called extrusion multiplier. Tune it after temperature, not before.

    G-code¶

    The line-by-line instructions the printer follows: move here, heat this, extrude that. You rarely write it by hand.

    Gyroid¶

    A wavy infill pattern that is strong in more than one direction and prints without crossing itself. A good default when you do not want to think about it.

    Hardened nozzle¶

    A steel or harder tip that survives carbon-fiber and glass-fiber filament. Plain brass wears out quickly on those.

    Heat creep¶

    Heat traveling too far up the hotend so filament softens and jams before the melt zone. Classic PLA failure inside a hot closed box.

    Heated chamber¶

    An enclosure with its own heater, not just leftover heat from the bed. The spec that matters is the maximum chamber temperature, often 55–65 °C on current machines that publish one.

    Hotend¶

    The heated block, heat break, and nozzle that melt the filament. The nozzle temperature you set is the hotend temperature.

    Hygroscopic¶

    Absorbs water from the air. Wet PETG, TPU, and nylon pop, string, and print weak. Dry them.

    IDEX¶

    Independent dual extruders. Two heads that can print two materials, or copy and mirror a part. The Snapmaker J1s is an IDEX machine.

    Infill¶

    The lattice inside the part. 15% is plenty for many decorative prints. Functional parts often want more walls before they want more infill.

    Input shaping¶

    A motion calibration that cancels vibration so high speed does not leave ripples (ringing) on the walls.

    Ironing¶

    A second slow pass of the nozzle on the top surface to smooth it. It adds time and can scar the top if the flow is off.

    Layer height¶

    How thick each slice is. 0.20 mm is a common everyday height with a 0.4 mm nozzle. Thinner looks smoother and takes longer.

    Layer shift¶

    A whole layer (or the rest of the print) steps sideways. The nozzle usually hit something, or a belt skipped.

    Live Z¶

    Adjusting the nozzle height while the first layer is printing. Prusa's name for a Z-offset tweak you can do in real time.

    Nozzle¶

    The small metal tip the melted plastic leaves through. 0.4 mm is the usual size. Smaller nozzles show more detail and clog more easily.

    Orientation¶

    Which way the part sits on the plate. It changes strength, supports, and the look of the layers. Layers are weakest between each other.

    Overhang¶

    A slope or underside with nothing under it. Gentle slopes print in mid-air. Steep ones need support or a smarter orientation.

    PEI¶

    A plastic coating on many build plates. Textured PEI grips PLA well. Smooth PEI can grip PETG so hard that you want glue as a release layer.

    Perimeter / wall¶

    The outlines of each layer. More walls make a part stronger faster than adding infill.

    PETG¶

    Tougher and a bit more heat-tolerant than PLA, and more likely to string. It can stick too well to smooth PEI.

    PID tuning¶

    A calibration so the hotend or bed holds a steady temperature instead of swinging. Most modern printers do this themselves.

    PLA¶

    The easy filament. Stiff, detailed, and happy with lots of cooling. It softens in a hot car. See Materials 101.

    Pressure advance¶

    A calibration that starts and stops extrusion pressure so corners are not blobby. Bambu calls a related feature flow dynamics. PrusaSlicer calls it linear advance.

    Prime tower / purge¶

    Extra plastic printed beside the part so a filament change or a second nozzle starts clean. Single-nozzle color changes waste more than a tool changer.

    PTFE tube¶

    The slippery white tube filament slides through. The ends wear out and cause clicks or under-extrusion. Cut replacements square.

    Raft¶

    A thick disposable platform printed under the whole part. Use it when the part itself will not stick. A brim is usually enough and wastes less plastic.

    Resin printing¶

    A printer that cures liquid resin with light, usually layer by layer in a vat (SLA or MSLA). Finer detail than FDM, and a different safety routine: gloves, washing, and curing.

    Retraction¶

    A short pull-back of filament before a travel move, so the nozzle does not dribble a string.

    Ringing / ghosting¶

    Ripples on the walls after a corner. Speed, acceleration, or a loose belt. Input shaping is the usual fix on printers that have it.

    Seam¶

    The little mark where each layer's outline starts and stops. You can hide it on a corner. You cannot delete it.

    Shell¶

    The solid outside of the part: walls plus top and bottom layers. The shell is what you see and what takes most of the load.

    Shore hardness¶

    How soft a flexible filament is. Shore A and Shore D are different scales. Compare the number only within the same scale, on the spool's data sheet.

    Skirt¶

    A loop printed around the part that does not touch it. It primes the nozzle so the real print does not start with an empty line.

    Slicer¶

    The program that turns a 3D model into layer instructions (G-code or a printer project). Bambu Studio, PrusaSlicer, OrcaSlicer, and Creality Print are common ones.

    Spaghetti¶

    A bird's nest of filament because the part came off the plate or a layer printed in the air. Stop the print. The nozzle is fine; the part is not.

    Spool¶

    The reel the filament is wound on. Cardboard and plastic spools both work, but some multi-material units dislike warped cardboard.

    Stepper motor¶

    The motor that moves an axis in small counted steps. If it skips, you get a layer shift or a missed step you can sometimes hear as a thud.

    STL¶

    A model file that is only the shape. No color, no plates, no printer settings.

    Stringing¶

    Fine hairs of plastic between parts. Often wet filament or a nozzle that is too hot, not a broken printer.

    Support¶

    Sacrificial plastic that holds up overhangs. It leaves marks. A second material that does not stick to the part makes removal much easier.

    Thermal runaway protection¶

    Firmware that shuts the heater off if the temperature does not rise as expected. Leave it on. It is a fire safeguard.

    Tolerance¶

    How much bigger a hole or gap needs to be so two printed parts fit. A 0.2 mm gap per side is a common starting point for a snug fit. Test it.

    Tool changer¶

    The printer swaps whole toolheads or hotends instead of purging one nozzle. Less wasted plastic, more moving parts. Snapmaker U1 and Flashforge Creator 5 Pro ship with four toolheads.

    TPU¶

    Flexible filament. Print it slower. Soft grades often cannot go through a filament changer.

    Under-extrusion¶

    Not enough plastic comes out. Walls look thin, gappy, or weak. Causes include a partial clog, printing too fast, or a tangled spool.

    VOC¶

    Volatile organic compounds, gases released when plastic is hot. ABS and nylon are the ones to take seriously. See Safety.

    Volumetric flow¶

    How many cubic millimeters of plastic the hotend can melt per second. Printing faster than that limit causes under-extrusion no matter what the speed slider says.

    Warping¶

    Corners lifting off the plate as the plastic cools and shrinks. Common with ABS, ASA, nylon, and PC. An enclosure and a brim help.

    Z-banding¶

    Repeating horizontal ridges up the walls. Often a mechanical Z-axis problem, not a filament setting.

    Z-offset¶

    How far the nozzle sits from the plate on the first layer. Too high and lines do not stick. Too low and they squash into elephant's foot or grind the plate.
